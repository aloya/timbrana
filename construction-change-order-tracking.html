Construction change order tracking that keeps the whole story together.

A construction change order starts before anyone approves a price. A field condition, scope clarification, owner request or subcontractor conversation raises a question: what changed, who needs to price it, and who is responsible for the next decision?

Timbrana helps project managers and superintendents follow that sequence without losing the original issue, discussion, notes or next action. It's project awareness for the parts of construction that often fall between documentation and follow-through.

What should a construction change order workflow track?

  1. Origin and scope. Record the field issue or client request, what is changing and why it matters.
  2. PM review. Confirm the proposed change is ready to price and identify any missing information.
  3. Trade partner pricing. Request pricing from the relevant subcontractor and track who still owes a response.
  4. Pricing review. Review the received amounts and document the proposal to be presented.
  5. Client decision. Track whether a change is awaiting approval, accepted, declined or needs another answer.
  6. Connected follow-through. Keep resulting tasks, communications and field progress attached to the change.

The key question is not just “What is the change order status?” It's also “Who has the next move, what are we waiting for, and what happened to get us here?”

Why spreadsheets and disconnected email chains become difficult

A spreadsheet can show a change order number, dollar amount and status. It often does not explain the conversations that created the change or which field task must follow an approval. When the relevant email is in one inbox, the field photos are in another folder, and a follow-up is only a personal reminder, the project team has to reconstruct the record.

A connected workflow keeps the change order alongside its originating issue, pricing requests, decisions and relevant project activity. That helps the superintendent understand whether to proceed with work and helps the PM see which decision is blocking progress.

Construction change orders and RFIs are related—but different

An RFI requests clarification. A change order documents a proposed change to the agreed scope, cost or time. A field question may lead to an RFI first, and its answer may reveal a change requiring pricing. Those records should remain connected without being treated as the same approval.

Frequently asked questions

How should a superintendent report a potential change?

Start with the site condition, location, relevant photos and what work is affected. Identify the responsible PM and any trade partners involved. That information becomes the foundation for a review and, where necessary, a change order.

When should a change order be considered approved?

Only after the authorized client decision is documented under the project's contractual approval process. A subcontractor's price is not the same as client approval.

What makes change order tracking useful to the field?

The field team needs more than a list of amounts. It needs to know whether work may proceed, what is waiting for authorization, who is responsible and where related photos, calls or tasks can be found.

Project scenario · Field condition

The field condition does not match the drawings. Is it an issue, an RFI or both?

The field team often discovers the condition first. The project needs to preserve what was observed while moving the formal question to the person who can answer it.

Construction project scenarioUpdated September 28, 2026

Start with the condition

Capture what is physically present, where it occurs and why it conflicts with the planned work. Photos and field notes belong with this original observation.

Use an issue while the path is still being determined

An issue is useful when the team first needs to investigate responsibility or decide whether formal clarification is required. It keeps the unresolved field condition visible before an RFI exists.

Create the RFI when a formal answer is needed

When the question requires design clarification or an authoritative project response, create the RFI from the issue rather than retyping the field condition. The original evidence should remain linked.

Shift responsibility clearly

Once the RFI is sent, the project may now be waiting on the design team rather than the superintendent. That handoff should be visible so the team knows who has the next move.

Bring the answer back to the field

When the response arrives, connect the direction to the original condition and the affected work. If the answer changes cost or scope, the next path may be a change order rather than immediate correction.

The goal is one understandable project thread.

Keep the original condition or communication, the current responsibility and the next action connected instead of recreating the same item in multiple places.

Why this is useful in Timbrana

Timbrana lets the field condition begin as an issue and move into an RFI without losing the photos, location, discussion or reason the question was raised.

No re-entering the field conditionThe RFI can carry forward the original issue context instead of forcing the superintendent or PM to recreate the story.
Responsibility changes visiblyThe project can show when the field has done its part and the next answer now belongs to the design team or another responsible party.
The answer can keep movingIf the RFI response creates cost, scope or field work, the next workflow can remain connected to the same originating condition.

What this means for the company

The company spends less time rebuilding field context when a condition moves from the superintendent to the PM and then to the design team. Photos, location, the original issue and the formal question stay connected as responsibility changes.

Faster handoffsThe PM can act on the field condition without asking the superintendent to recreate the same information in another form.
Less information lossThe reason the RFI was raised stays attached to the formal answer and the work it affects.
Better downstream decisionsIf the response creates cost or schedule impact, the company already has the originating condition and answer in one thread.

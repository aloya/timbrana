The distinction

Records tell you what was entered. Awareness tells you what matters now.

Construction teams already create records: emails, reports, RFIs, change orders, photos, calls, schedules, and notes. Those records are important, but they do not automatically create a current understanding of the project.

Timbrana focuses on the relationships between those moments — what they changed, what is still unresolved, and what somebody needs to do next.

The goal is not more information. It is a clearer understanding of the information already being created.

Role-appropriate awareness

The same project. Different responsibilities.

A superintendent, project manager, owner, and company leader should not have to read the same screen or follow every conversation to understand the project.

Timbrana keeps the underlying project story connected while bringing forward the part each person needs: today's field concerns, decisions waiting on direction, owner questions, unresolved responsibilities, or higher-level areas that may need attention.

SuperintendentWhat changed, what needs attention, and what needs to happen in the field.
Project ManagerDecisions, commitments, unresolved items, and where follow-through is needed.
OwnerRelevant progress, questions, decisions, requests, and what happens next.
LeadershipWhere projects are moving and where intervention may be useful.

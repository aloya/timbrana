Construction RFI tracking

Track construction RFIs from field question to usable direction.

An RFI is more than a question sent to the design team. Construction RFI tracking should preserve why the question arose, who owes an answer, what direction was received and what field work changes next.

For superintendents and project managersPublished November 14, 2026

When does a field issue become an RFI?

A superintendent may discover that an opening conflicts with the drawings or that a detail cannot be built as shown. Start by documenting the observed condition, location and reference drawing. The project manager can determine whether the question requires a formal request for information (RFI), internal coordination or a different workflow.

What belongs in a useful RFI record?

Specific questionAsk for the missing direction without burying the request in a long narrative.
Source contextInclude relevant plans, specifications, photos, location and the actual field condition.
Current responsibilityShow who must respond and how the team will follow up on an unanswered question.
Received directionPreserve the response, date and relationship to the original condition.
Field follow-throughIdentify what work can resume or what additional decision is required.

How should unanswered RFIs be managed?

A status such as “open” is not enough when work is waiting. Capture the responsible reviewer, the affected scope and the consequence of no response. The superintendent should be able to explain what is held up; the project manager should be able to determine when to follow up or escalate. Avoid claiming an RFI has been answered merely because a message was sent.

What if an RFI response changes scope or cost?

Design direction and commercial approval are different decisions. If the response changes scope, the project manager may need to initiate a change-order review while keeping the RFI and originating condition connected. The field team should not treat an RFI response as automatic authorization to incur additional cost.

Example: Door opening conflicts with a detail

The superintendent records the actual opening and references the drawing. The PM routes a focused question, tracks the pending answer and shares the resulting direction with the affected trade. Any cost or schedule impact is handled in its appropriate workflow.

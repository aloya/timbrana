Project scenario · Damaged delivery

The delivery arrived, but part of it is damaged. Is the delivery complete?

Physical arrival does not always mean the project received usable material. The delivery event and the resulting issue are related but describe different project facts.

Construction project scenarioUpdated September 28, 2026

Record what actually arrived

Mark the delivery according to what occurred and note the damaged, missing or rejected material. Photos taken at receipt are valuable evidence.

Create an issue for what remains unresolved

If replacement material, vendor action or trade follow-up is required, create an issue connected to the delivery. That keeps the original arrival record intact while tracking the unresolved condition.

Assign the current responsibility

The next move may belong to the supplier, trade partner, PM or superintendent depending on who must arrange replacement or approve a solution.

Track the replacement as a new commitment

Once a replacement date is promised, schedule that return delivery. The project can then distinguish the original damaged delivery from the corrective commitment.

Close both records appropriately

The first delivery remains a historical fact. The issue closes when the material problem is resolved, and the replacement delivery closes when the usable material actually arrives.

The goal is one understandable project thread.

Keep the original condition or communication, the current responsibility and the next action connected instead of recreating the same item in multiple places.

How this fits Timbrana

Timbrana is designed so everyday project activity can move into the workflow that owns the next step while preserving where the item came from. That can mean an issue, scheduled event, owner question, RFI, change order or personal follow-up depending on what the project actually needs.

Construction daily logs

What should a construction daily log record — and what should happen after it?

A useful daily log does two jobs: it preserves what happened on the jobsite and makes it obvious when something from that day still needs action.

For superintendents and project managersUpdated September 28, 2026

What belongs in a construction daily log?

The daily log is the project’s day-by-day field record. It should capture enough detail that someone can understand the condition of the job later without turning the log into a transcript of the entire day.

Trade partners and laborWho was on site, crew size or labor hours when useful, and the scope each trade was working on.
Work performedWhat actually happened in the field, organized by trade, area or scope rather than a generic “worked on project” note.
ProgressMeaningful movement on tasks or areas where percent complete or status helps the team understand what remains.
Field conditionsConditions that affected the work, access, sequencing, inspections, deliveries or another trade.
PhotosVisual evidence that adds context to the day: completed work, existing conditions, concealed work, deliveries or an issue that may matter later.
Commitments and follow-upsCalls, promised return dates, owner questions or other items that should not disappear when the log is submitted.

Do not turn every daily log entry into a task

Most daily activity is routine. A contractor shows up, completes planned work and leaves. That belongs in the daily record, but it does not need a second workflow.

The exception is when an entry changes the project picture. A missing material delays another trade. An owner asks a question that needs a decision. A field condition may require an RFI. A subcontractor commits to return on Thursday. Those items need a path beyond the daily log.

Keep the log simple. Carry forward only what still needs attention.

This prevents the project from becoming a duplicate set of logs, tasks, calls and reminders that all describe the same thing.

What should carry forward from the daily log?

A practical rule is to ask whether the item is complete when the day is over. If the answer is no, identify the workflow that owns the next move.

Field issueCreate an issue when a condition needs correction, investigation, responsibility or follow-through.
RFIUse an RFI when the project needs a formal clarification from the design side or another responsible party.
Change order pathCarry the item into pricing and approval when the condition changes scope or cost.
Owner questionSeparate a client-facing decision from internal field coordination so the owner sees only the part that requires their involvement.
Scheduled return, inspection or deliveryPut time-based commitments on the project schedule instead of burying the date inside narrative notes.
Personal follow-upUse a personal reminder when the next action belongs only to the individual, not the whole project team.

What makes a daily log useful to the office?

The office usually does not need more prose. It needs enough field context to understand why something changed and whether it requires action. A strong daily log makes the important exceptions easy to distinguish from routine work.

For a project manager, that means being able to see the field record and then move directly into the issue, RFI, owner question or change path without asking the superintendent to explain the same condition again.

Daily log mistakes that reduce project awareness

Writing one paragraph for the whole project

Separate activity by trade, area or subject so the record remains understandable later.

Recording a commitment without a date or owner

“Plumber will return” is weaker than recording who committed, what they are returning for and when that return is expected.

Using the daily log as the only place an open issue exists

The daily log should preserve the day. An unresolved condition should also live where the team can manage its current status and next responsibility.

Duplicating the same item everywhere

One connected item is easier to understand than a daily note, separate task, call note and reminder that all have to be updated independently.

The goal: a record of the day that stays useful tomorrow

A construction daily log should not become a second project-management system. It should be the field record that captures the day and provides a clean starting point when something needs to continue.

That is the difference between documenting activity and maintaining Project Awareness: the record explains what happened, while the connected workflow shows what that activity means now.
